Bandwidth allowances at Hetzner and DigitalOcean: reading the fine print

CPU and RAM are what people compare first, but traffic allowances can matter more for apps that serve files or media. The two providers handle it differently. Check their pricing pages before deciding, because numbers change, but the structure has been stable for a while.

Hetzner Cloud

Cloud servers in the EU locations include a large monthly traffic allowance, measured on outgoing traffic, with incoming traffic free. US and Singapore locations include much less. Overage is billed per TB at a low rate. For most small apps the allowance is effectively unlimited.

DigitalOcean

Each droplet includes an outbound transfer allowance that scales with the plan size, and allowances are pooled across droplets in the same account. Overage is billed per GB. Inbound is free.

What this means in practice

  • A typical web app with API traffic and a few images will not get close to either allowance.
  • File downloads, video or backups served to users can exceed a small DigitalOcean allowance and turn into a real line item, while staying inside Hetzner's EU allowance.
  • Location matters. A Hetzner server in a US location has a smaller allowance than one in Germany or Finland.

Measure your own number

Run vnstat on the current server for a month, or read egress from your existing provider's billing page. Multiply by your expected growth and compare against each plan.

Use a CDN for static assets

Putting heavy static files behind a CDN reduces origin traffic on either provider.
